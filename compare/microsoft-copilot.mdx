Microsoft 365 Copilot is the AI assistant built into Microsoft 365: Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Outlook and Teams, grounded in your tenant's mail, files and meetings. PipesHub is an open-source (Apache 2.0) context layer that indexes apps across vendors and serves that index to people and to agent harnesses over MCP. For most Microsoft 365 organizations the question is not which one, but whether PipesHub covers something Copilot doesn't.

<Note>
  Microsoft facts are dated **as of November 2026** and linked under [Sources](#sources). See [How PipesHub compares](/compare/overview) for the summary across all products.
</Note>

## When Microsoft 365 Copilot fits better

- **Your work lives in Microsoft 365.** Copilot works inside the Office apps and Teams, with your mail, calendar, meetings and SharePoint content. PipesHub has no Office add-ins and does not index Microsoft Teams (Teams is an agent [action](/toolsets/microsoft-365/teams)).
- **You want governance in tools you already run.** Copilot uses Microsoft 365 permissions and works with Microsoft Purview and Entra. PipesHub's open-source edition has its own sign-in (password, email one-time code, Google, Microsoft); SAML, OIDC and SCIM are in the [Enterprise edition](/editions).
- **You want a large connector catalog without running servers.** Microsoft offers more than 100 prebuilt Copilot connectors, plus custom connectors. PipesHub has 35+ indexed sources and, self-hosted, your team operates it.
- **You want an established vendor.** Microsoft provides enterprise support and contracts. PipesHub has no usage or adoption analytics.

## When PipesHub fits better

- **Content must stay on infrastructure you control.** PipesHub runs with Docker Compose or Helm in your cloud, data centre or an [air-gapped network](/deployment/vendor/private). Copilot runs in Microsoft's cloud.
- **You want to choose the models.** PipesHub works with about 20 LLM providers, including local [Ollama](/ai-models/llm/ollama) and [vLLM](/ai-models/llm/vllm). Copilot uses models Microsoft selects (OpenAI and Anthropic models); Microsoft notes that Anthropic models are hosted outside Microsoft and are excluded from the EU Data Boundary.
- **Your agents run outside Microsoft 365.** PipesHub's [MCP server](/mcp/overview), listed on the official MCP registry, gives Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Gemini CLI and other clients permission-aware search over the same index.
- **You want no per-seat fee.** The open-source edition has no licence cost; you pay for infrastructure and model usage.

## Deployment and data control

PipesHub stores indexed content, permissions and embeddings in databases you run. Data leaves your deployment only for the services you configure, such as a hosted model provider, plus product telemetry, which is on by default and can be turned off. See [Security and privacy](/security-and-privacy).

Copilot keeps connector content in your Microsoft 365 tenant, in Microsoft's cloud. Microsoft also controls oversharing risk with tools such as Purview DSPM for AI and Restricted Content Discovery.

Both products depend on source permissions being right. If a SharePoint site is open to everyone, both will show its content to everyone.

## Pricing model

As of November 2026, Microsoft lists Microsoft 365 Copilot at $30 per user per month paid yearly ($31.50 paid monthly on an annual term), on top of a qualifying Microsoft 365 subscription. Copilot Business for organizations of up to 300 users is reported at $21 per user per month. Copilot Studio agents use Copilot Credits, reported at about $0.01 per credit pay-as-you-go or $200 for a 25,000-credit pack. Microsoft also sells Microsoft 365 E7 at $99 per user per month, which includes Copilot.

PipesHub's open-source edition has no licence or seat fee. Your costs are servers and model usage; local models remove per-token charges.

## Working alongside Copilot

Most organizations run more than one assistant, and Gartner reports that only a small share standardize on one. Common ways to use PipesHub with Copilot:

- **Keep Copilot for Microsoft 365, use PipesHub for the rest**, for example self-hosted Jira and Confluence Data Center, file shares, or databases, or teams who use other assistants.
- **Give coding agents company context.** Developers using Claude Code, Cursor or Codex connect to PipesHub over [MCP](/mcp/overview) with a [personal access token](/developer/personal-access-tokens) or OAuth 2.0.
- **Serve restricted networks.** Where a hosted assistant isn't allowed, PipesHub with local models runs fully inside the network.

PipesHub can index Microsoft 365 too, through its [SharePoint](/connectors/microsoft-365/sharepoint), [OneDrive](/connectors/microsoft-365/one-drive) and [Outlook](/connectors/microsoft-365/outlook) connectors.

## Where PipesHub is weaker

- No Office app integration, and Microsoft Teams messages are not indexed.
- Sync is scheduled. A deletion or removed permission that the source does not report is cleaned up only on the next complete full sync; scheduled runs are not full syncs.
- Some team connectors (S3-compatible storage, Azure Blob, SMB shares, Notion) can't share per-item permissions. See [Permissions and freshness by connector](/connectors/permissions-matrix).
- SAML, OIDC and SCIM are in the [Enterprise edition](/editions), not open source. No usage analytics and no scheduled agents.
