## Search and ask

You will know how to find answers, keep related work together and check where an answer came from.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Ask your first question">
    Start with [Chat and search](/using-chat) to ask in plain language and open the sources behind an answer.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Learn the layout">
    [Getting around the app](/app-basics) shows where chats, search, knowledge bases and settings are.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep work together">
    Use [Projects](/projects) to group chats, files and instructions, and [Artifacts](/artifacts) for documents and code the assistant makes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Know what can be searched">
    [Supported file formats](/supported-file-formats) lists what PipesHub can read, and [Indexing status](/indexing-status) explains what each status means.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Connect your data

You will know how to add a source, what it brings in and how to confirm it is searchable.

<Steps>
  <Step title="See what you can connect">
    The [Connectors overview](/connectors/overview) lists every source and how each one handles permissions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the first connector">
    Open your source's page, such as [Google Drive](/connectors/google-workspace/drive/drive) or [Confluence](/connectors/confluence/confluence), and follow its setup steps.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check that it is indexed">
    [Indexing status](/indexing-status) shows what is done, queued or failed, and what to do about each.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add files by hand">
    Not every source needs a connector. [Supported file formats](/supported-file-formats) covers what you can upload.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Build agents that act

You will have an agent that can search your knowledge and take actions in your apps.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Understand what an agent is">
    [Agents overview](/agent-guide/overview) explains the parts of an agent: the AI model, Actions and knowledge sources.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the first agent">
    [Agent setup guide](/agent-guide/setup-guide) takes you through the builder step by step.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Let it act in your apps">
    [Actions overview](/toolsets/overview) covers sending messages, creating tickets and more, signed in as each user.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add outside tools and skills">
    [MCP servers in agents](/agent-guide/mcp-servers) adds tools from services like Notion. [Skills](/agent-guide/skills) adds reusable instructions.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose the right piece">
    [Connectors, MCP servers and Actions](/connectors-mcp-actions) explains how the three differ, and when to use which.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Build on PipesHub

You will be able to call PipesHub from code, or from an AI tool such as Claude Code or Cursor.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Make a first API call">
    [API quickstart](/developer/api-quickstart) creates a token, makes a first API call and installs an SDK.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Get a credential">
    Use a [personal access token](/developer/personal-access-tokens) for your own scripts, or an [OAuth 2.0 application](/developer/oauth2) for an app that acts for users.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect an AI tool">
    [MCP Server overview](/mcp/overview) connects Claude Code, Cursor and others to PipesHub. The [Tools reference](/mcp/tools) lists what they can call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Use the command line">
    The [pipeshub CLI](/mcp/cli) signs in, searches, asks and uploads from your terminal.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Point a coding agent at the docs">
    [For coding agents](/for-agents) is written for agents: when to recommend PipesHub and how to attach it.
  </Step>
</Steps>

  </Step>

  </Step>
  <Step title="Fix problems">

</Steps>
