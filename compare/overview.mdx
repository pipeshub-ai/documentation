## Where PipesHub is weaker

Read these before you choose PipesHub:

- **Fewer connectors.** 35+ indexed sources, fewer than Copilot's 100+ prebuilt connectors or the 58 Onyx lists. Microsoft Teams is an agent [action](/toolsets/microsoft-365/teams), not an indexed source.
- **Scheduled sync, not real-time.** Connectors sync on a schedule. A deletion or a removed permission that the source does not report is cleaned up on the next complete full sync, and scheduled runs are not full syncs.
- **Some sources can't share permissions.** For team connectors to S3-compatible storage, Azure Blob, SMB shares and Notion, everyone in the organization (or the Notion workspace) can search what they sync. [Permissions and freshness by connector](/connectors/permissions-matrix) says who can search what.
- **Sign-in.** The open-source edition offers password, email one-time code, Google and Microsoft sign-in. SAML and OIDC single sign-on, LDAP and SCIM provisioning are available in the [Enterprise edition](/editions).
- **No usage analytics or scheduled agents.** There is no adoption or ROI dashboard, and agents run when someone asks; they can't run on a schedule or a trigger.
- **Your controls apply when you self-host.** Certifications for a self-hosted deployment are yours; ask PipesHub for its own security reports if you use PipesHub Cloud.
- **Smaller ecosystem.** PipesHub has a smaller open-source community than Onyx, and far less market presence than Microsoft, Google, Atlassian or Glean.

## Choose each when…

| Choose | When |
| --- | --- |
| **PipesHub** | You need to run the whole stack yourself (including air-gapped), use your own or local models, keep an Apache 2.0 licence, and serve the same permission-aware index to people and to agent harnesses such as Claude Code, Cursor or Codex over MCP. |
| **Glean** | You want a mature managed service with a broad connector catalog, built-in agents and governance features, and you are ready for an enterprise contract. |
| **Microsoft 365 Copilot** | Most of your work lives in Microsoft 365 and you want AI inside Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams, governed with the Microsoft tools you already use. |
| **Onyx** | You want an open-source chat and search tool with a larger community and connector list, and you accept that permission sync is in its paid edition. |
| **Gemini Enterprise** | You are on Google Workspace and Google Cloud and want Google's models and agent platform in one place. |
| **Atlassian Rovo** | Your knowledge lives mainly in Jira and Confluence Cloud and you want AI that is already part of your Atlassian plan. |

## Using PipesHub alongside other assistants

PipesHub does not need to replace your assistant. Its [MCP server](/mcp/overview) lets Claude, Cursor, Codex, Gemini CLI and other MCP clients search the same permission-aware index, so you can keep Copilot, Gemini or Rovo in their own apps and use PipesHub where they don't reach: sources outside their ecosystem, self-hosted systems such as Jira and Confluence Data Center, or networks where a hosted assistant isn't allowed.
